# ASSUMPTIONS

Every constant in this repository is one of two things:

* **measured** — published, or read out of a source file at build time by a
  script anyone can re-run. Cited where it is used.
* **an assumption** — listed here, numbered, with a reason and with the test
  that will catch it if it is wrong.

There is no third category. In particular there is no category for "it made the
walk look better", which is what the deleted body model was full of.

Step 1 introduces no constants of its own: everything `tools/step1_anatomy.py`
prints is read out of the MJCF or out of BANC, and everything `tools/step1_sim.py`
does is MuJoCo integrating the model as shipped.

**Current total: 22.** Six arrive with step 2, the first step that simulates
the nerve cord rather than measuring it. Eight more arrive with step 3, the
first step that joins that cord to a body — which means joining two vocabularies
(BANC's muscles, flybody's joints) and inventing a transducer between what the
body does and what the organ reports. Three more arrive with step 4, which
drives six legs at once and then has to admit what it had to leave out to do
it.

| 26 | odor is a field, and where it is | a kinematic plume in the world frame: source, wind, widening, decay. **The geometry constants are ENGINEERING PLACEHOLDERS** (`tools/odor_field.py`): decay Λ = 1.20 cm, source width σ0 = 0.045 cm, widening α = 0.22 per cm, near-source smoothing s0 = 0.05 cm, wind 0.30 cm/s, and the source is 2.5 mm in front of the animal with the air blowing onto it, so the plume passes over its head | the brief asks for odor as a spatial *and temporal* field, and a fly's walking speed is ~1 cm/s, so a plume that decays over a body length or two is the scale at which the animal can be inside it | the field's own properties are measured in the same tool and gated in CI: the time-mean falls monotonically with distance (0.473 → 0.001 over 0.05–3.2 cm), nothing reaches upwind (exactly 0.0000), the antennae are inside a filament 20 % of the time and in clean air 81 %. **Nothing else about it is claimed** — it is not a solution of the advection–diffusion equation. |
| 27 | odor is intermittent, not smooth | filaments with a duty cycle D = 0.20, passing at 3.5 Hz, with a downwind phase lag of 6.0 rad/cm; concentration inside a filament is a raised cosine to 2/D = 10× the mean, zero outside | an antenna in a real plume spends most of its time in clean air and meets odor as discrete packets — that is the property insect odor navigation is built on (Murlis, Elkinton & Cardé 1992, *Annu. A sinusoid around the mean cannot produce it: it is above any threshold more than half the time | measured at the antennae: peak/mean **10.0**, inside a filament **20 %** of the time, in clean air **81 %**. The **constant** `intermittency` of the first version was a sinusoid and produced a 50 % duty, which the tool's own gate would have caught had it not been replaced by the duty-cycle form first. |
| 28 | concentration → receptor drive | `drive = 45 × c / (1 + c)`, in the app's tone units: a saturating map, one constant (`baseline_drive`) | the LIF fires when its membrane passes 1.0 and a group's membrane tracks its external current, so the constant has to be *measured* against that threshold rather than picked: a ladder of currents on the ORN group puts the group's departure from its ~21 Hz clean-air rate at a current of about 2–3. It is **an APPROXIMATION** of a receptor's dose–response, not a measured one | yes, and it is the correction that mattered most: with the first version's 2.6 the current at the animal's own antennae peaked at 1.37 — *below* the threshold — so the app shipped a fly that could not smell its own food, and the gate still passed, because its strongest condition multiplied the drive by five, which the app never does. | 29 | the cells that smell | `sensory_olfactory` = BANC's `cell_function == "olfactory"`, 3,000 cells in the packed binary, driven as one group by the field at the antennae | it is BANC's own annotation of which cells are olfactory, the same source every other sensory group in this repository comes from | `tools/odor_field.py --gate` fails if the group is missing or empty, and the count is printed along with where the smell goes first — 26,359 synapses onto 881 cells, 615 of them not labelled olfactory, read out of the packed connectome rather than assumed. | 30 | the haltere stroke | ±45° about each haltere's hinge at 200 Hz, with the two halteres **in antiphase** (`stroke_hz`, `stroke_deg`, `convention` in `tools/haltere_gyro.py`) | the body model's halteres are **passive stubs** — one hinge joint, no actuator, no stroke (`data/flybody/…/fruitfly.xml`: `<joint name="haltere_left"/>`, and no haltere entry in the actuator table) — so the stroke cannot come from the model. The frequency is *Drosophila*'s wingbeat, ~200 Hz (Lehmann & Dickinson 2005, *J. Exp. Biol.* 208:3075), the arc ~90° is Pringle's measured haltere stroke (1948, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 233:347) | the tool *measures* what follows from it rather than assuming it: the hinge→CoM distance (0.172 mm) and mass (0.822 µg) are read out of the MJCF, and the resulting forces are printed as a fraction of the in-plane force the same haltere feels every stroke — **0.35 % at 100 °/s**, which is the discrimination problem Pringle posed and the reason the sensor is specialised. The antiphase convention is the one degree of freedom the model cannot decide (a hinge axis has no direction): under it yaw and roll land in the pair's difference channel to **99.7 %** and pitch in the sum channel to 99.9 %; under the opposite convention yaw moves into the sum channel and the gate fails. Both are run; the report says so. |
| 31 | what a haltere senses | `drive = bias + gain · (Ω · a)/100 °/s` per haltere, with `a = v̂ × n̂` the sensitivity axis (v̂ = the direction the haltere's centre of mass moves, n̂ = its hinge axis), `bias` 3.0, `gain` 2.0, and rates below 1 °/s treated as zero | the Coriolis force on a horizontally oscillating mass is `F = 2 m (Ω × v)`, which is **linear in Ω** — that is why a haltere is an angular-*rate* sensor and not an accelerometer — and the campaniform fields at its base are oriented to transduce the out-of-plane component (Pringle 1948; Nalbach 1993, *J. Comp. Physiol. A* 173:509, for the kinematics that make dF2 the field that sees it). The map from that strain to a receptor current is an APPROXIMATION, as in assumption #28 | the axes are **measured from the body model** (cos(a_left, S·a_right) = 1.0000, i.e. the two are plain mirrors), the channels are gated (yaw in the difference, pitch in the sum, ≤0.4 % leakage), and the LIF response is measured: a 400 °/s yaw moves the difference between the two pools by +79.8 Hz, a 400 °/s pitch moves their *sum* by +40.4 Hz and their difference by only −6.0 Hz. The `bias`/`gain` are ENGINEERING PLACEHOLDERS in the app's tone units; the resolution they produce (~10 °/s at 1 s) is measured, not picked. |
| 32 | the halteres' two populations | `sensory_haltere_left` = 216 cells, `sensory_haltere_right` = 212, BANC's own annotation, driven as two independent channels because the pair's *difference* is the yaw signal | this is the one place in the repository where a left/right comparison exists: unlike the olfactory cells (0 of 3,000 carry a side), BANC annotates the haltere afferents by side, and 57 cells are innervated by both — enough to subtract on | read out of the packed connectome on every run: the afferents' synapses land on 738 cells (left) and 647 (right); their **direct** targets include `motor_neck` 31, `motor_wing_steering_left` 8, `motor_wing_steering_right` 10 and `motor_haltere` 18 cells. **The two pools are not interchangeable** — at rest the right fires ~48 Hz and the left ~23 Hz on the same drive — so every rotation is measured against that baseline, which is a property of their different connectivity rather than noise (it reproduces across seeds to a few Hz). |
| 33 | reading a rate out of 216 cells | the yaw channel is the left-minus-right rate; a turn counts as resolved when the response is monotone in the rate and above three standard errors of the still animal's own difference, measured on the same window | the pools are small and the LIF is a threshold device, so at very small drives individual cells cross threshold and the response is quantised (at 5 °/s the difference moves +8.2 Hz, more than at 10 °/s). Gating on a rate rather than on the drive is what makes that visible instead of hidden | measured: the drive is monotone in the yaw rate at every rung (exact algebra) and the rate response is monotone from 10 °/s up; the smallest turn the pair resolves in 1 s is **10 °/s** (0.17 rad/s), with the 5 °/s rung printed and marked as not evidence. **Not claimed:** that the animal uses this to walk straight — item 17 is where walking is. |


**Not modelled at all, and therefore not claimed:** gap junctions (electrical
synapses), neuromodulation, synaptic plasticity, and any per-connection weight
that is not the synapse count. Step 4 additionally does not model the
connections *between* the legs: each leg's cord is separate, so whatever
coordination a real tripod gait needs has to come out of the body, the floor
and the shared descending tone — and, as the recording shows, it does not. Muscle *dynamics* is modelled as the one-pole
filter of assumption #7 and nothing more — no force–length, no force–velocity,
no series elasticity. The body's actuators are the affine position actuators
`flybody` ships, so the model inherits their stiffness as if it were muscle.

## Removed, and why

The whole previous stack was deleted in the commit that created this file. The
constants it carried — `thrustPerHz`, `yawPerHz`, `walkPerHz`, `turnPerHz`,
`liftThresholdHz`, the arousal time constant, the habit-layer bout rates, the
tangent gait gains — were gains chosen so that a hand-imposed oscillator would
produce motion that looked like walking. They are preserved in the
`archive/round5-world` branch. None of them are coming back; behaviour in this
repository is produced by circuits, not by a schedule.
