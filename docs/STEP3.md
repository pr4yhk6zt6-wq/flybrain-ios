# Step 3 — the loop closes

Step 2 produced a number: an imposed movement changes the firing rate of the
leg's motor pools, through the wiring of the connectome. Step 3 spends that
number. The pools drive the muscles, the muscles drive the joints, the joints
drive the sense organs, and the organs drive the pools back — and then the leg
is pushed, and the question is whether it pushes back.

Everything below is generated by `tools/step3_closedloop.py` and lives in
[`reports/step3_closedloop.md`](../reports/step3_closedloop.md); the run also
writes a machine-readable `reports/step3_closedloop.json`, and CI asserts on
that file rather than on anything written here.

---

## The short answer

1. **The animal still stands**, with one leg's three proximal joints under
   connectome control: the COM moves 1.2 µm over the second half of a 1.5 s
   run, with an sd of 1.6 µm, and 5.8 of 6 feet stay down.
2. **The loop resists imposed movement.** At κ = 4 — an organ whose working
   range is a quarter of the joint's — imposed extension is resisted at
   ρ = +0.125 ± 0.018 and imposed flexion at ρ = +0.186 ± 0.016, both resolved.
   At κ = 1, the anatomically-scaled gain, the effect is real in the flexion
   direction and sits under the noise floor in the extension direction.
3. **The resistance is the connectome's, not the wiring's.** Reverse the
   organ's published transduction polarity and the reflex reverses too:
   −0.073 ± 0.028 and −0.087 ± 0.033, both resolved, both now *assisting*.
   One biological sign flips and the behaviour follows.
4. **The switch the reflex literature predicts — resistance turning to
   assistance under load — did not appear.** Loading the leg to twice the
   animal's weight leaves the reflex resisting at both loads and in both
   directions. Nothing in this step assisted except the polarity-reversed
   control and the saturating end of the κ sweep.

## 1. What had to be joined

Two vocabularies meet here and neither is wrong: BANC names **muscles**,
flybody names **joints**. Three tables carry the join, and all three are in the
tool rather than in prose so that they can be checked.

**Muscle → joint** (`POOL_ACTION`). The trochanter is fused to the femur in
*Drosophila*, so its muscles act on the coxa–femur joint, not on a joint of
their own. Each joint is then driven across its whole anatomical range and the
interior angle decides which way flexion points — measured on the assembled
animal, not assumed:

| joint | actuator | flexion is | pools that raise qpos | pools that lower it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `coxa_abduct_T1_left` | coxa_abduct | +q | coxa rotator post | coxa rotator ant |
| `femur_T1_left` | femur | −q | trochanter extensor | trochanter flexor, femur reductor |
| `tibia_T1_left` | tibia | −q | tibia extensor | tibia flexor |

**Pool rates → joint command.** Each joint gets one commanded angle from its
antagonists' balance:

```
b    = ā_raiser / (ā_raiser + ā_lowerer)          ā = the pool's rate, filtered at τ = 60 ms
ctrl = q_rest + (the joint's anatomical range) × (b − b_ref)
```

The joint's own range is the only scale the body model supplies, and writing it
as a ratio buys something real: step 2's weakest assumption — the arbitrary
synaptic scale N* — cancels out of the command. The N* sweep confirms it: the
standing pose and the reflex do not march with N*.

`b_ref` is not a parameter. It is **measured** by an iteration: run standing,
measure each joint's balance, move `ctrl` towards the command that balance
implies, run again — three passes — until the pose the cord commands and the
pose the cord produces are the same pose. That fixed point is the animal's
stance, found rather than chosen.

**Joint → organ.** Two channels, and neither has a free gain in it:

```
chordotonal  = tone × (1 − polarity × κ × x)     x = (knee angle away from stance) ÷ (half the joint's range), clipped
campaniform  = tone × (this leg's vertical ground-reaction force ÷ the force it carries standing)
```

The campaniform one is a strain gauge: it reads the leg's own measured force
against the force that same leg carries in the stance it just found, so no
constant has to be invented. The chordotonal one has exactly one unknown, κ,
and it is swept.

## 2. Does it stand?

| | |
|---|---|
| COM height, first → last | −0.1635 → −0.2628 mm |
| COM change over the second half | **−0.00117 mm** |
| COM sd over the second half | 0.00155 mm |
| knee tremor (sd) | 0.0407 rad |
| knee drift over the run | +0.0027 rad |
| feet on the floor | 5.81 of 6 |
| verdict | **STANDING** |

Step 1's animal stood because every joint was commanded to the pose of the
scan. Here, one leg's three proximal joints are commanded by the motor pools of
the connectome, one millisecond at a time, and the rest of the animal is left
where step 1 left it.

The joint is not still. It trembles with an sd of **0.041 rad**, and that is
reported rather than smoothed: the tibia extensor pool is **two neurons**, at
the rates the standing cord produces it fires a handful of spikes per muscle
time constant, and the commanded angle carries that quantisation. A real muscle
filters harder than τ = 60 ms does, and a real joint has its antagonist's
stiffness across it.

One leg carries **24 %** of the animal's weight standing, and **60 %** when the
extra two body weights are hanging from the coxa. The standing pool rates are
the cord's own answer to what a stance is:

|---|---:|

| tibia extensor | 12.12 |

## 3. Push the knee

An external torque about the knee for 500 ms, calibrated so that it moves the
passive leg **0.478 rad**. ρ = 1 − |closed| / |passive|: positive means the leg
moved less than it would have with the organs disconnected, so it **resisted**.

**At κ = 1**, the organ spanning the joint's whole anatomical range:

| load | pushed | passive Δq | closed Δq | ρ | resolved? | verdict |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| 0 W | extension | +0.4596 | +0.4628 | −0.007 ± 0.054 | no | no reflex |
| 0 W | flexion | −0.4766 | −0.4454 | **+0.065** ± 0.046 | no | resists |
| 2 W | extension | +0.4373 | +0.4435 | −0.014 ± 0.016 | no | no reflex |
| 2 W | flexion | −0.2135 | −0.1576 | **+0.262** ± 0.112 | yes | resists |

**At κ = 4**, where the effect is big enough to read:

| load | pushed | passive Δq | closed Δq | ρ | ρ per unit organ drive | resolved? | verdict |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| 0 W | extension | +0.4631 | +0.4052 | **+0.125** ± 0.018 | +0.089 | yes | resists |
| 0 W | flexion | −0.4791 | −0.3900 | **+0.186** ± 0.016 | +0.128 | yes | resists |
| 2 W | extension | +0.4239 | +0.4085 | +0.036 ± 0.026 | +0.028 | no | resists |
| 2 W | flexion | −0.2254 | −0.2118 | +0.060 ± 0.037 | +0.088 | no | resists |

A result is called resolved only when it clears two standard errors under
**both** ways of estimating them — the spread across repeats, and the spread of
the seed-matched closed-minus-clamped differences. The two estimators agree
closely about ρ and disagree about the size of its error, so the stricter one
decides.

### The switch that was looked for, and did not appear

This was the question the step was built to ask: does resistance turn into
assistance when the leg is loaded? **No.** Every one of the eight conditions
above resists or is unresolved; not one assists. Loading made the measured ρ
*smaller* at κ = 4 (0.125 → 0.036, 0.186 → 0.060), but the last column shows
why that is mostly mechanics and not circuitry: a loaded leg is stiffer, the
same torque moves it 0.23 rad instead of 0.48, so the organ is stimulated half
as much. Divide by the stimulation it actually received and the load effect
largely disappears (0.089 → 0.028, 0.128 → 0.088).

That is a negative result and it is published as one. It is also the honest
reading: with one joint pushed, one leg driven, and a campaniform channel that
is a single scalar, this experiment is not yet the one in which a fly changes
its mind about how to use a leg.

## 4. What settles it: the polarity control

| transduction polarity | extension ρ | flexion ρ |
|---|---:|---:|
| flexion stretches the organ (published) | **+0.125 ± 0.018** | **+0.186 ± 0.016** |
| extension stretches the organ (reversed) | **−0.073 ± 0.028** | **−0.087 ± 0.033** |

Both rows are the same code, the same gain, the same muscle filter, the same
command law, the same standing reference. The only thing that differs is the
sign of one published biological fact — whether flexing the tibia stretches the
femoral chordotonal organ or relaxes it. With the published sign the loop
resists; with it reversed the loop assists; and all four numbers are resolved.

So the resistance is not something the wiring of this loop manufactured. It is
what the connectome does with the organ's real transduction, and if the
organ's sign were the other way round the animal would behave the other way
round.

Splitting the feedback into its two channels does **not** decompose the effect:
at κ = 1 the chordotonal channel alone gives −0.003 ± 0.047 and +0.016 ± 0.088,
and the campaniform channel alone gives −0.020 ± 0.015 and +0.029 ± 0.012 —
each within its own error of zero. At the anatomically-scaled gain the two
together are needed, and it is their combination the main table measures.

## 5. How sensitive is it to what we chose

| κ (fraction of the joint range the organ spans) | extension ρ | flexion ρ |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | +0.002 ± 0.086 | +0.027 ± 0.051 |
| 4 | **+0.125 ± 0.018** | **+0.186 ± 0.016** |
| 16 | −0.034 ± 0.014 | +0.080 ± 0.040 |

The dose–response is not monotone and the top of it is a warning, not a
result. At κ = 16 the drive is `tone × (1 − κx)` floored at zero, so a push of
half a radian silences the organ entirely — the saturating regime of the
transducer, not a property of the cord. That is why κ = 4, a working range a
quarter of the joint's, is where the readable answer comes from, and it is a
fair description of a range-fractionated organ.

| muscle filter | command jitter | extension ρ | flexion ρ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 ms | **0.3517 rad** | +0.032 ± 0.061 | −0.100 ± 0.294 |
| 60 ms | 0.0964 rad | −0.007 ± 0.054 | +0.065 ± 0.046 |
| 200 ms | 0.0312 rad | +0.077 ± 0.055 | −0.027 ± 0.067 |

τ_muscle is not free either. At 20 ms the joint chases individual spikes and
the commanded angle swings by 0.35 rad; at 200 ms the tremor is gone and the
answer is again inside its own error. 60 ms was chosen **before** any of these
numbers existed, from the twitch-fusion time of insect skeletal muscle, and
the sweep is reported precisely because the choice matters.

| N* (step 2's synaptic scale) | knee at standing | command jitter | extension ρ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | −0.0059 rad | 0.0210 | +0.034 |
| 12 | +0.0267 rad | 0.0964 | −0.085 |
| 24 | −0.0250 rad | 0.0000 | −0.025 |

At N* = 24 step 2's cord falls silent, the command law degenerates to the rest
pose, and the loop goes with it — which is what a ratio-based command is
supposed to do, and is why the scale is reported as a sweep rather than fixed
and forgotten.

## 6. What came out wrong

Written down rather than tuned away, per the project's own rule.

* **Extension at κ = 1 shows no reflex at all** (−0.007 ± 0.054 at 0 W,
  −0.014 ± 0.016 at 2 W). Not resolved, not tuned, not hidden: it is in the
  table. The asymmetry with flexion is real in the numbers and unexplained —
  the transducer is symmetric in κ, so the rectification is in the cord.
* **The channels do not add.** Neither alone reproduces the effect; together
  they do. Anything claiming to have isolated "the chordotonal contribution"
  from these numbers is over-reading them.
* **The standing balance does not track load.** `b_ref` comes out identical to
  four decimal places at 0 W and at 2 body weights
  (coxa_abduct 0.175, femur 0.2542, tibia 0.8227), even though the leg's
  carried force goes from 24 % to 60 % of body weight. It should not be
  insensitive, and it is not yet understood.
* **`b_ref` is read off a fixed settle time** and is sensitive to it, which the
  fixed-point iteration on `force_ref` fixed for the force but not for the
  balance.
* **The knee tremor is 0.041 rad** with two neurons in the tibia extensor pool.
  Some of what looks like a small ρ may be the loop fighting its own quantised
  command.
* **One leg, three joints, no tarsus.** The other five legs, the claw and the
  tarsus stay frozen at step 1's rest command throughout.

---

## 7. What this step establishes, and what step 4 owes

**Establishes:** the loop can be closed between a connectome-derived cord and a
physics body without a clock, a trajectory, or a trained network anywhere in
it; the animal still stands with a real leg under real control; and the reflex
that comes out is a resistance reflex whose sign is set by the organ's
published transduction — proven by reversing that one sign and watching the
behaviour reverse with it.

**Does not establish:** that the fly can walk. One leg is driven. The switch
from resistance to assistance under load — the thing step 3 was built to look
for — was not found, and is reported as not found.

**Step 4 owes:** all six legs driven from their own pools, so the animal can
carry its own weight distribution instead of leaning on five frozen ones; the
tarsus and claw, which is where a real stance is decided; a muscle model with
dynamics rather than a one-pole filter; an explanation of why the standing
balance ignores load; and more repeats, because at κ = 1 the honest answer is
that four trials cannot resolve an effect of 0.07.
