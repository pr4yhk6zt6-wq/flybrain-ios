# Step 5 — the body, as physics the phone runs

Generated by `tools/build_body.py`, `tools/fly_aba.py`, `tools/pack_body.py`.
Reports: `reports/step5_aba.json`. Code: `FlyBrain/Sources/FlyDynamics.swift`.

## What this step is for

Steps 1–4 ended with a recording. `tools/step4_world.py` ran six cords cut out of
the BANC connectome, turned their motor pools into joint commands, moved the body
with them, and wrote `world/frames.bin` — and the app played that back, frame by
frame. It looked like a fly and it was not one: the animal in the app had no
mass, no floor and no muscles. It could not be pushed, it could not fall, and it
could not surprise anyone.

Step 5 removes the recording. What runs now is a solver: it is told a torque at
each of the 102 joints, and the pose is whatever the physics says. The order of
authority is the one the project is committed to — nervous system first, physics
before graphics — and the graphics are now downstream of the state rather than
the state being downstream of the graphics.

## The three checks (`python3 tools/fly_aba.py --verify`)

| check | what it proves | result |
|---|---|---|
| forward kinematics against `mj_forward` | the tree, the joint axes, the frame conventions | 4.4e-16 cm / 2.0e-15 |
| 20 ms of free fall, zero torque, no floor, side by side with MuJoCo stepped with the same 100 µs Euler integrator | the spatial algebra, the inertia model, gravity, the integration | first step 1.7e-08 rad / 1.7e-04 rad/s; body positions 2.1e-05 cm; centre of mass 7.0e-09 cm |
| the animal standing for 3 s on muscles alone | that the muscle calibration is enough to hold the animal up | see `reports/step5_aba.json` |

The middle check is reported rather than asserted at the end of the run, and the
reason is worth stating plainly: the fly's joint inertia is the model's own
1e-6 g cm² armature at the lightest joint, against accelerations that reach
6.8e5 rad/s². A quantity like that is the outcome of a cancellation across the
whole chain, and *nobody* reproduces it to machine precision from published
quantities — measured, MuJoCo's own step disagrees with its own `mj_fullM` +
`qfrc_smooth` by 4e-2 relative on `haltere_left`. What is asserted is the first
step, the body positions and the centre of mass, which are what gravity acts on
and which stay exact. The joint rates then separate at the system's own
amplification rate, 1.04x per step.

## What the solver is

`tools/fly_aba.py` (NumPy) and `FlyBrain/Sources/FlyDynamics.swift` (the phone)
are the same algorithm, written twice:

* **Featherstone's articulated-body algorithm**, with the velocity-product
  acceleration in both passes. Leaving it out of the first pass is the most
  expensive mistake in the file: the recursion looks consistent, the static pose
  is exactly right, and every moving pose is quietly wrong.
* **Gravity as a force on every body**, not as an acceleration handed to the
  root. The two agree for a fixed base and every textbook writes it the other
  way round; for a free root they differ, and the difference is invisible in
  every static test and wrong in every falling one.
* **Dampers folded into the articulated-body inertia** (`D = Sᵀ Iᴬ S + armature
  + damping·dt`), which is what makes a damper on a 1e-6 g cm² joint integrable
  at 100 µs at all.
* **A floor of penalty contacts**, one per collision geom, with an exact support
  function per shape. A convex body's lowest point is its centre minus its
  support along the plane normal, so all 74 geoms are tested every substep with
  no pair-wise geometry. `CONTACT_STIFFNESS = 150` is *fitted*: the animal weighs
  0.966 g cm/s², and its own contacts carry that weight at a total penetration of
  6.36e-03 cm, so a spring that carries the same weight at the same depth is
  150. The damper is 0.005 and the number to remember is its stability limit,
  `b < m·2/dt`: at 0.5 the animal is thrown clear of the floor.
* **Muscles as antagonist pairs**, force-based: a Hill force-length curve whose
  width comes from the joint's own anatomical range, and a velocity term applied
  *per direction* — the shortening muscle weakens with speed, the lengthening one
  is loaded and gets stronger. Applying one shortening curve to both removes the
  braking torque, and a joint that cannot brake flies apart in 200 µs.
* **The file's own actuator law** for what each muscle can do: `max_torque =
  |gainprm[0]|·max|ctrlrange|` and `passive_stiffness = -biasprm[1]`, straight out
  of the MJCF, 62 joints of 102. The other 40 have no actuator in the file and
  their limits are marked `placeholder` in the asset with the source named.

## What is live in the app now

`FlyBrain/Sources/FlyLiveBody.swift` runs the solver on the display link and
hands the poses to `FlyWorld.apply(live:)`, which poses the 85 mesh nodes by
name. The Map screen no longer reads `frames.bin` at all: the recording is
optional in the build, and the transport bar shows simulated time, substep
count, substep size and the measured real-time factor instead of a scrub slider.

The stance the app starts from is measured by `tools/stand_body.py`, which
stands the animal up *in this solver* (see `reports/step5_aba.md`): the MJCF
stance is a stance for MuJoCo's soft contacts, and on a penalty floor the load
divides differently — 0.42 of the weight on one claw instead of 0.23 — which is
0.01 of torque on a 1e-6 g cm² joint, and enough to push the animal off the
floor. The asset therefore carries `stance_q`, `stance_root_z` and the measured
`stance_excitation`, and the app holds the posture the animal actually stands at.

The animal's current drive is **muscle tone** — the excitation that holds the
posture its own actuators were measured holding — so it stands, and the floor,
the mass distribution and the joint springs all have to be right at the same
time for it to stay standing. It is not yet *behaving*: the excitation offset
per joint comes from `FlyLiveBody.drive`, which is where the cord goes next, and
until then the HUD says so rather than implying otherwise.

Two honest gaps, both declared rather than hidden:

* **The stance servo is a test harness.** Nothing in the app holds a pose; the
  servo exists so the physics can be measured on an animal that is not falling
  over, and its gains are read off the discretisation (`omega_dt²·I/(dt²·cap)`,
  `kd_dt·I/(dt·cap)`) rather than tuned.
* **The app runs the physics, not yet the loop.** Connecting
  `SimulationEngine`'s motor pools to `FlyLiveBody.drive` is step 6, and it is the
  step that turns standing into walking.
